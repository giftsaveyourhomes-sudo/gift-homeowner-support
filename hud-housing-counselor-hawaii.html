What Is a HUD-Approved Housing Counselor, and Why Does It Actually Matter?

The moment a homeowner falls behind, the phone starts ringing and the mailbox fills up with offers of "help" — most of it from companies trying to sell something. Somewhere in the middle of that noise is a genuinely free, federally vetted resource that a lot of people never realize exists: the HUD-approved housing counselor. It's worth understanding exactly what that is, because it's different from almost everything else you'll be offered.

What "HUD-approved" actually means

A HUD-approved housing counselor works for a nonprofit agency that has been reviewed and certified by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development specifically to provide housing counseling. This isn't a marketing label a company can slap on itself — it's a federal certification tied to specific standards, training, and oversight. Critically, these counselors don't work for your lender, and they don't get paid based on selling you a particular product or outcome. Their job is to help you understand your actual options.

What a counseling session actually looks like

  • A full review of your financial picture — income, expenses, the specifics of your mortgage, and what led to the hardship.
  • An honest walkthrough of every realistic option — loan modification, repayment plans, forbearance, selling the home, or in some cases bankruptcy — without steering you toward whichever one benefits the counselor.
  • Help communicating with your servicer, including understanding paperwork and deadlines that can feel overwhelming on your own.
  • In some cases, direct involvement in mediation or negotiation with your lender on your behalf.

This is typically done over the phone, video, or in person, and — this is the part that surprises people most — it's free, funded through HUD rather than charged to you.

How this is different from a "foreclosure rescue" company

This distinction matters enormously, and it's exactly the gap that predatory companies exploit. A for-profit "rescue" company often charges upfront fees, pressures you to sign quickly, and may steer you toward a specific product (like a refinance or a quick sale) that benefits them financially — sometimes at your expense. A HUD-approved counselor has no such conflict of interest built in. If you want the fuller picture of the scam tactics circulating right now, I've written about that separately.

How to find a legitimate one on Oʻahu

  • Use HUD's official counselor search at hud.gov rather than a general web search, which can surface paid ads from companies that aren't actually HUD-approved.
  • Confirm the agency's HUD approval directly if you found them another way — a legitimate counselor won't hesitate to confirm this.
  • Watch for red flags — any "HUD-approved counselor" asking for payment upfront, guaranteeing a specific outcome, or pressuring you to sign something immediately is behaving in a way real HUD counselors don't.

When it's worth reaching out

Earlier is always better than later, but there's no wrong time. Whether you've just started falling behind, received a formal notice, or are deep into a foreclosure timeline, a HUD-approved counselor can still help you understand where you stand and what's realistically left to do.
