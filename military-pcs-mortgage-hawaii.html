Military PCS Orders and Your Mortgage in Hawaiʻi: What SCRA Actually Protects

With so many families stationed on Oʻahu, PCS orders are one of the most common reasons a homeowner here ends up asking, "What happens to my mortgage now?" If you've heard that the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA) can help, that's true — but the protections are more specific than most people assume. Here's what SCRA actually covers, and what it doesn't.

What SCRA is for

The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act is a federal law designed to ease financial and legal burdens on active-duty servicemembers so military service doesn't create a crisis at home. For homeowners, its best-known mortgage-related protections include interest rate caps on debts taken out before active duty, and added foreclosure protections during (and for a period after) active-duty service.

What SCRA does not automatically do

  • It doesn't erase your mortgage debt. Payments are typically reduced or protected in specific ways — not eliminated.
  • It doesn't apply the same way to every loan. Coverage details depend on when the loan originated and the specifics of your orders.
  • It isn't automatic in every case. Some protections require notifying your servicer and providing a copy of your orders — this isn't something that happens in the background without you taking action.

PCS orders and selling vs. renting out the home

When orders come through, homeowners on Oʻahu generally weigh a few paths: selling before the move, renting the home out while stationed elsewhere, or requesting a period of forbearance if the timing is tight. Each comes with its own mortgage, tax, and logistical considerations — there's no single right answer, and what fits depends on your specific orders timeline, your loan terms, and whether Oʻahu's rental market works for your situation.

VA loans have their own considerations

If your mortgage is a VA loan, there are additional considerations specific to VA guidelines — including how occupancy requirements interact with a PCS move. Your loan servicer and the VA are the right source for how those rules apply to your specific loan.

Where to get the specifics

  • Your installation's legal assistance office (JAG). Free to active-duty servicemembers and often the fastest way to get an SCRA notice reviewed correctly.
  • Your mortgage servicer, directly. SCRA protections generally need to be requested with a copy of your orders — ask specifically what they need from you.
  • Military OneSource or your branch's relief society for broader financial counseling tied to a PCS move.

Getting the right help

SCRA is a federal legal protection, and how it applies to your specific mortgage is a legal question best answered by your base legal assistance office — that's not something I can interpret on your behalf. What I can help with is the practical side: understanding your options if a PCS move is colliding with a mortgage payment you're worried about, whether that's forbearance, renting the home out, or selling, so you walk into that conversation already oriented.
